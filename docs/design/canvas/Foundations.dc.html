Elevation — five heights, five meanings
Height is information, not decoration. A thing that floats higher is either closer to your hand or more urgent. Every raised surface also carries a one-pixel top lip of white, which is what makes it feel like an object rather than a rectangle.
recessed
0 · Page & wells
Canvas, inputs, table headers, progress tracks. Inset shadow, never a border.
1 · Resting card
Data tables, panels, KPI tiles, nav items at rest.
2 · Under the cursor
Hovered rows and cards lift; that lift is the only hover affordance needed.
3 · Floating control
Menus, popovers, the bulk-selection bar, toasts.
4 · Dialog
Modals and sheets. Only one may be open, and it dims what is behind it.
House rules
Nine decisions already made, so they never have to be argued again.
No hard borders. Separation comes from a tonal step, an inset hairline at 7% ink, or a shadow. A 1px solid line is a bug.
Money is monospaced. Always tabular, always right-aligned, decimals one step lighter than the integer part.
Quiet until touched. Secondary controls sit flush with their surface and only gain contrast on hover, focus or selection.
One primary per view. Exactly one gradient-filled purple button is visible at a time; everything else is a surface.
Status is a word. Colour ranks it, the label says it, a dot or glyph confirms it. Never colour alone.
Empty is an em dash. Missing data reads "—" in ink 3, never a zero, never a blank cell.
Mobile is not a narrow desktop. Tables become prioritised cards; toolbars become sheets; the primary task gets a 50px button at the bottom of the screen.
Icons are drawn, not typed. 1.7–1.9px stroke on a 24 grid, currentColor, no emoji anywhere.
Density is the feature. A 38px row that stays readable beats a 56px row that looks calm in a screenshot and exhausts you by noon.
